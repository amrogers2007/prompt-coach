# Startup Glossary (plain English)

Terms you'll hear constantly. No jargon-shaming — everyone had to learn these.

- **Startup** — a company built to grow fast by solving a problem in a new way.
- **B2B vs B2C** — Business-to-Business (you sell to companies) vs
  Business-to-Consumer (you sell to individual people). *Your idea is B2B.*
  B2B is harder to sell but each customer pays much more.
- **Enterprise** — big companies (thousands of employees). Big budgets, but slow
  buying, lots of approvals, security reviews. You saw this at State Farm.
- **SaaS (Software as a Service)** — software people pay for on a recurring
  subscription instead of buying once. Most startups today are SaaS.
- **Per-seat pricing** — you charge per user (e.g. $5 per employee per month).
- **ARR / MRR** — Annual / Monthly Recurring Revenue. The predictable
  subscription money coming in. The number investors care about most.
- **ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)** — a precise description of the *best* type of
  customer to go after first. Narrow beats broad.
- **The buyer vs the user** — in B2B they're often different people. The boss
  buys it; the employee uses it. You must satisfy both.
- **Wedge** — the small, sharp first version of your product that gets you in the
  door. You don't launch the whole vision at once; you launch the wedge.
- **MVP (Minimum Viable Product)** — the smallest thing you can build that lets
  you learn whether people actually want it. On purpose embarrassingly small.
- **Customer discovery** — talking to potential users *before* building, to learn
  their real problems. The single most important early-startup skill.
- **The Mom Test** — a famous little book/idea: ask about people's actual past
  behavior, not hypothetical futures. "Would you use this?" → useless (people
  lie to be nice). "Walk me through the last time you struggled with X?" → gold.
- **Validation** — evidence that a real problem exists and people want it solved.
- **Traction** — proof it's working: users, usage, revenue, signed customers.
- **Churn** — how many customers cancel. High churn = the product isn't sticky.
- **Pilot / POC (Proof of Concept)** — a small paid or unpaid trial with one
  company to prove value before a full contract.
- **Moat / defensibility** — what stops a competitor (or Microsoft) from copying
  you. Every investor will ask this.
- **Runway** — how long your money lasts before you run out. (Not urgent for you
  yet — you have no costs.)
- **Bootstrapping** — growing using your own money/revenue, without investors.
- **Venture Capital (VC) / raising** — taking investor money in exchange for part
  ownership (equity), to grow faster. Not a step-one thing for you.
- **Accelerator / incubator** — programs (e.g. Y Combinator) that give small
  funding + mentorship to early startups. Colleges also often have their own.
- **Pitch** — a short, clear explanation of the problem, your solution, and why
  it matters. You'll get good at this by doing it a lot.
