# Action Plan — cheapest, most-learning-first

> The #1 beginner mistake is building the product first. Don't. Building is the
> expensive, slow way to learn something you can learn this week for free by
> talking to people. Order matters: **learn → prototype → build.**

---

## Phase 0 — Sharpen the thinking (this week, ~free)
- [ ] Finish filling in [IDEA.md](IDEA.md) and [OPEN-QUESTIONS.md](OPEN-QUESTIONS.md).
- [ ] Write your **one-liner** so a stranger gets it in 10 seconds. Rewrite 5x.
- [ ] Pick your **first ICP guess** (one specific type of company + one specific
      type of employee). Narrow. You can be wrong — you just need a starting point.

## Phase 1 — Customer discovery (next ~2 weeks, ~free, highest value)
This is where you'll learn the most. Goal: understand the real problem before
building anything. **Use your State Farm + Harvey Mudd network — this is your
superpower.**
- [ ] List **10 people** who either (a) were handed AI at work and struggle, or
      (b) manage a team and care about AI adoption. (Ex-State Farm colleagues,
      managers, family, friends' parents, etc.)
- [ ] Interview **5–7 of them.** Use *The Mom Test* style — ask about their real
      past behavior, not hypotheticals:
      - "Tell me about the last time you used AI for work. What happened?"
      - "What's frustrating about it? When did you last give up on it?"
      - "How did you learn to use it — did anyone teach you?"
      - "If your company offered training, would people actually use it? Why not?"
      - (For managers) "How do you even know if your team is using AI well?"
- [ ] After each chat, jot 3 notes: their real problem, a surprising quote, and
      whether this confirms or breaks your assumptions.
- [ ] **Decision point:** do 3+ people clearly have this pain? If yes → Phase 2.
      If not → adjust the idea based on what you heard. (This is a *win*, not a
      failure — you just saved yourself months.)

## Phase 2 — Tiny prototype (a few weekends, fun + great for your resume)
Build the smallest thing that *shows* the coaching idea. You have the skills.
- [ ] Recommended first build: a **browser extension** that watches a web AI chat
      (like claude.ai / chatgpt.com) and pops a tiny, relevant tip. Even faking
      the "smart" part at first (hard-coded tips triggered by keywords) is enough
      to show people and get reactions.
- [ ] Show it to the same people from Phase 1. Watch their face. Do they lean in?
- [ ] This alone is a fantastic portfolio project even if the company never happens.

## Phase 3 — First proof someone wants it (later)
- [ ] Try to get **one pilot** — a team willing to try it (even free) and give
      feedback. A team lead at a company you know is the dream first "customer."
- [ ] The magic moment: someone says "when can we actually use this?" or "would
      you take money for this?"

## Phase 4 — Only if there's real pull
- [ ] Explore college entrepreneurship resources (Harvey Mudd / Claremont
      Colleges have startup programs, advisors, sometimes small grants).
- [ ] Look into accelerators (incl. student-focused ones) — later, not now.

---

## What to ignore for now (common beginner traps)
- ❌ Company name, logo, LLC paperwork, domain — irrelevant until people want it.
- ❌ Raising money / investors — way premature.
- ❌ Building the "full" polished product — you'll build the wrong thing.
- ❌ Worrying that Microsoft might copy you — worry about that once you have users.

## The single most important thing
**Go talk to 5 real people who have this problem.** Everything else follows from
what you hear. Nothing you build matters until you've done this.
